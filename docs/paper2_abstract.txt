LLM pipelines for applicant ranking combine free-text assessment with iterative listwise comparison, needing extra hallucination-mitigation machinery and no confidence signal. In September 2026, TypeSafe AI released Jev, the first "System One Model," a non-autoregressive architecture answering typed questions in one parallel pass with calibrated per-answer confidence, removing free-text hallucination and schema violation by design. This paper aims to deploy Jev in a real applicant-ranking pipeline through three contributions: criteria-grounded question design, single-pass cost/latency efficiency over an iterative tournament, and repeat-to-repeat reliability testing. The research method is transforming an existing LLM-and-tournament pipeline into a fully structured typed-question architecture, evaluated on 273 shortlisted (job, applicant) pairs across 10 job profiles against the closest prior system. Criteria-grounded design raises per-requirement confidence from 0.672 to 0.894 and pooled seniority/education confidence from 0.635 to 0.766. The pipeline needs under half as many model calls (273 versus roughly 2,061), shows lower per-candidate latency (16.5s versus 74.3s), zero-priced output, and confidence of 0.894 versus the prior system's Faithfulness of 0.880. Repeat-to-repeat tests show ranking stability matching the prior system (Kendall's τ=0.965 versus ≈0.957). A non-autoregressive structured-decision model can equal free-text assessment reliability at lower cost and latency, given matching question design, efficiency, and stability.
